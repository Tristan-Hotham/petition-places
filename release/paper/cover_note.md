## Summary of the contribution (for a cover letter)

Research on e-petitions has asked who signs and whether petitions influence Parliament; this paper asks where they are signed. Using signature counts for all 650 constituencies on the 734 petitions to the UK Parliament elected in 2024 that reached about 3,000 signatures, and an analysis plan written before any outcome was computed, it measures each petition's geography against each seat's overall signing with empirical Bayes location quotients. Every petition map is spatially clustered (median Moran's I 0.48); petitions about particular places are much more concentrated than national ones; and two dimensions, aligned with the education and age divides of recent British electoral geography, hold 30% of the variation between seats and are almost identical across petitions created in different periods (r = 0.96). The 2024 vote explains about a quarter of a typical petition's map, less than census composition. EU petitions are robustly under-signed where Reform UK polled strongly; a planned link for immigration petitions is fragile because that family pools opposite aims. Data and code are openly released.
